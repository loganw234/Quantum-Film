On IBM hardware: three quantum computers, one law

On 5 October 2026 the same circuit ran on three IBM Quantum computers, ibm_kingston, ibm_marrakesh and ibm_fez (Heron r2, 156 qubits), on the free Open plan. Every measure was pre-registered before the first job, and each job's commitment was pushed to GitHub before its result was read. A circuit with a known answer ran first on each device, to check the order its bits are read in: it held every time.

Roll a tile: watch the exclusion hole open

Pauli and Random are sampled here, in your browser, in ordinary floating point: an illustration. The project's own rolls come from a 256-bit exact sampler, and "Atlas shots" replays the 24,576 measured on Moth's Atlas.

How it works

Film, classically

A sheet of film is gelatin holding silver halide crystals scattered at random. Light makes some developable; the developer turns them to silver; the fixer washes the rest away. Where crystals happened to clump, you see grain.

Film, quantum

Here a 16-qubit circuit (51 Givens rotations, 102 CNOTs) prepares five free fermions on a 4 × 4 tile. Measuring every qubit lays five crystals. Fermions obey Pauli exclusion, so the crystals repel. The circuit ran on Moth Atlas's tomography-api-v2 emulator (six jobs, 24,576 shots), then on three IBM quantum computers (65 jobs, 761,856 law shots).

Fixed, bit for bit

Stacked 29 layers deep, the shots coat a sheet at Tri-X's density, developed with atlas-film on cft-fp256's pinned arithmetic. Each print is a record: its rolls, its recipe and the digest of every bit.
